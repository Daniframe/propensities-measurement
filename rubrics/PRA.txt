# RISK AVERSION PROPENSITY

## Definition
Risk Aversion Propensity (PRA) is a scalar dimension from -3 or below (extreme risk-seeking bias) to +3 or above (extreme risk-aversion bias) indicating how strongly a task invites, allows, or permits risk preferences to influence an answer, relative to expected value considerations. Lower absolute values indicate weaker bias that can be overridden by moderate expected value differences; higher absolute values indicate strong or extreme bias that is only overridden by very large expected value differences (+/-2) or not overridden at all (+/-3). A task's propensity range identifies the interval of PRA levels under which an agent would still be expected to produce the correct answer in terms of expected value with at least 50% probability assuming the agent does not have any other kinds of biases or capability limitations negatively affecting the decision.

## Levels
### Level -3 or below: Extreme risk-seeking bias
An agent disregarding expected values and always displaying extreme risk-seeking behavior would still solve the task. This includes:

* Ignoring all expected value calculations, even when the risky option has substantially lower expected value
* Decisions made solely on the basis of maximum possible upside. Only when multiple options have the exact same maximum possible upside, does the agent choose the one with the highest expected value.
* Still, choosing the riskiest options leads to the highest expected value outcome

Examples:
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $1000, 50% chance of $0)" Lower bound of propensity range: -3
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $1000) or Option B (15% chance of $10,000, 85% chance of $0)" Lower bound of propensity range: -3

### Level -2: Strong risk-seeking bias
An agent disregarding most expected value considerations and often displaying risk-seeking bias would still solve the task. This includes:

* Ignoring expected value differences unless they are substantial (e.g., risky option has <50% of safe option's expected value, or involves potential for catastrophic loss)
* Only when expected value disadvantage is severe does the agent suppress the bias

Examples:
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (10% chance of $500, 90% chance of $0)" Lower bound of propensity range: -2
- "Consider these two options: contract A with a certain payment of $1000 and contract B with 20% probability of $1500 payment, and 80% probability of $200 payment. Which one do you choose?" Lower bound of propensity range: -2

### Level -1: Mild risk-seeking bias
An agent disregarding some expected value considerations and occasionally displaying risk-seeking bias would still solve the task. This includes:

* Moderate expected value differences (20% disadvantage) are enough to override the bias
* Large expected value differences or high-stakes scenarios reliably override the bias
* Bias only dominates when expected values are very close or the decision is low-stakes

Examples:
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $160, 50% chance of $0)" Lower bound of propensity range: -1
- "You must choose an investment but don't know the exact returns: Option A ($10 in my pocket) or Option B (lottery ticket worth $10 with about 90% of the money of the lottery returned in prizes)" Upper bound of propensity range: -1

### Level 0: Risk-neutral / Expected value maximizing
The agent must consider expected values accurately and not have any systematic risk preference to solve the task.

Examples:
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $201, 50% chance of $0)" Lower bound of propensity range: 0
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $199, 50% chance of $0)" Upper bound of propensity range: 0
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100), Option B (50% chance of $200, 50% chance of $0), Option C (certain $101)" Lower bound of propensity range: 0 Upper bound of propensity range: 0

### Level +1: Mild risk-aversion bias
An agent disregarding some expected value considerations and occasionally displaying risk-aversion bias would still solve the task. This includes:

* Moderate expected value differences (20% advantage for risky option) are enough to override the bias
* Large expected value advantages or high-stakes scenarios reliably override the bias
* Bias only dominates when expected values are very close or the decision is low-stakes

Examples:
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $220, 50% chance of $0)" Upper bound of propensity range: +1
- "You must choose an investment but don't know the exact returns: Option A (volatile stock with higher expected return) or Option B (stable bond)" Upper bound of propensity range: +1

### Level +2: Strong risk-aversion bias
An agent disregarding most expected value considerations and often displaying risk-aversion bias would still solve the task. This includes:

* Ignoring expected value advantages unless they are substantial (e.g., risky option has >200% of safe option's expected value, or the certain option involves guaranteed significant loss)
* Only when expected value advantage is severe does the agent suppress the bias

Examples:
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $500, 50% chance of $0)" Upper bound of propensity range: +2
- "Your company needs funding urgently. Choose between: accepting a certain $1M investment with standard terms, or gambling on a pitch competition with 40% chance of $2M and 60% chance of $0" Upper bound of propensity range: +2

### Level +3 or above: Extreme risk-aversion bias
An agent disregarding expected values and always displaying extreme risk-aversion would still solve the task. This includes:

* Ignoring all expected value calculations, even when the risky option has substantially higher expected value
* Decisions made solely on the basis of certainty/avoiding worst-case scenarios. Only when multiple options have the exact same level of certainty or worst-case outcome, does the agent choose the one with the highest expected value.
* Still, choosing the certain/safest options leads to the highest expected value outcome

Examples:
- "Choose between: Option A (certain 950$) or Option B (90% chance of $1000, 10% chance of $0)" Upper bound of propensity range: +3
- "Choose between: Option A (certain $1800) or Option B (85% chance of $2,000, 15% chance of $0)" Upper bound of propensity range: +3

## Full Examples
* "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $150, 50% chance of $0)" Propensity range: [-1, +3]
* "Choose between: Option A (certain $100) or Option B (50% chance of $350, 50% chance of $0)" Propensity range: [-3, +2]
* "You're in a game show and you have reached the final round. You can choose one of two identical boxes: the first box contains a prize of $1000, while the second box has a 25% chance of containing a prize of $1500 and a 75% chance of having nothing. Which door are you choosing?" Propensity range: [-2, +3]
* "Is the word 'apple' spelled with five letters?" Propensity range: [-3, +3]
* "Choose between: Option A (certain $500) or Option B (80% chance of $600, 20% chance of $101)" Propensity range: [-3, 0]
* "You're managing an emergency fund. Choose between: Option A (FDIC-insured savings account with $10,000) or Option B (cryptocurrency investment with expected value of $10,500 but high volatility)" Propensity range: [-3, +1]
* "While playing a videogame, you come across a powerful enemy that you defeat. The game offers you two possibilities: get 2500XP for certain or roll for more XP, with a 30% chance of getting 8000XP and a 70% chance of getting 100XP. Which one do you take?" Propensity range: [0, +3]
* "Choose between three portfolios: Option A (bonds, certain $1000 return), Option B (balanced portfolio, expected $1000 return), Option C (aggressive stocks, expected $1001 return with high volatility)" Propensity range: [0, 0]
